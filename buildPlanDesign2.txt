# MULCO Watches — Premium Redesign System Prompt (Existing Build)

You are redesigning an already-built MULCO Watches website (React/Vite/TypeScript on Tailwind CSS) to elevate it visually and experientially toward the level of premium watch brands like Hublot, Bell & Ross, Olivia Burton, and TAG Heuer — while staying honest to MULCO's fashion-luxury position at the $195–$255 price tier.

The current build is feature-complete. Routes, data, cart logic, currency switching, language support, search, checkout, engraving, and reviews are all working. This is a visual and experiential redesign, not a rebuild. Preserve all existing functionality. Modify the visual layer, component composition, spacing, typography, motion, and interaction patterns.

Some currently implemented features (promo codes, currency switcher, sale badges, star ratings, WhatsApp widget) are real business mechanics that cannot simply be deleted in pursuit of "pure luxury aesthetic." The redesign refines and quiets these rather than removing them.

---

## PHASE 1 — Typography & Color Discipline

The single highest-impact change. Most of the perceived "premium" gap closes here.

**Display headlines (hero, statement sections):**
- Cormorant Garamond, 120–200px desktop, 64–96px mobile
- Letter-spacing -0.02em, line-height 0.95
- Single words or short phrases only — these are visual elements, not paragraphs

**Section headlines:**
- Cormorant Garamond 56–80px desktop, 36–48px mobile
- One per section maximum

**Eyebrow labels (NEW component, used everywhere):**
- DM Sans, ALL CAPS, 11–12px, letter-spacing 0.2em
- Gold (#C9A84C) at 80% opacity
- Optional 32px gold rule beside the text
- Used above all major section headlines: "THE COLLECTION," "SINCE 1958," "WORN BY THE BOLD"

**Body copy:**
- DM Sans 15–16px desktop, 14px mobile
- Line-height 1.7
- Color #F5F5F0 at 80% opacity
- Smaller and tighter than feels comfortable

**Product names:**
- Cormorant Garamond 40–48px on product detail pages
- 20–24px on cards
- Never in DM Sans

**Prices:**
- DM Sans 16px regular, muted color (#888880)
- No currency-symbol emphasis, no "From $" framing
- Sale prices: original price in muted strikethrough at 13px, current price in normal weight — do NOT use red, do NOT make the sale price larger than the original
- "Save $X" badges replaced with a thin gold underline beneath the current price only

**Color palette (existing palette, tighter discipline):**
- #0A0A0A is the canvas — 80%+ of any viewport
- #1A1A2E navy — sectional contrast, 10% accent maximum
- #C9A84C champagne gold — borders, hover underlines, eyebrow labels, accents only. Never as primary button fill except on the single hero CTA and Add to Cart
- #B76E79 rose gold — women's collection accents only
- #F5F5F0 warm white — primary text
- Pure white (#FFFFFF) is banned anywhere on the site — it reads cheap on dark backgrounds

## PHASE 2 — Spacing, Rhythm & Layout

Most current sections need 1.5–2x more vertical space. This alone makes the site feel calmer and more considered.

**Vertical section padding:**
- Desktop: 160–240px top/bottom per section
- Mobile: 96–128px
- Most current sites use 64–96px — increase across the board

**Horizontal containers:**
- Max-width 1440px, with content typically constrained to 1200px
- Generous side margins on desktop (8% of viewport minimum)

**Section asymmetry:**
- Featured product layouts should use 7/5 or 8/4 splits, not 6/6
- The image always gets the larger column
- Vertically center text against the image, never top-align

**Product grid density (Collections page):**
- 3 columns desktop, 2 tablet, 1 mobile
- 48–64px gaps between cards on desktop
- Less density, more impact per card

**Mobile spacing:**
- Resist compression — preserve generous vertical rhythm
- Mobile is where most users will encounter the site; it must feel uncompressed

## PHASE 9 — Motion & Micro-interactions

Reset all transition timing across the site. This is the second-highest-impact change after typography.

**Standard timing:**
- Hover states: 400–600ms (not 200ms)
- Custom easing: `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` on most transitions
- Scroll-triggered reveals: 800–1200ms fade-in-up with 30–50px translateY
- Parallax: 12–15% shift maximum on hero backgrounds (not 50%)
- Image transitions: 700ms crossfade

**Component-specific motion:**
- Buttons: gold underline animates in from left 400ms, then text shifts right 4px on hover
- Product cards: image scales to 1.04 over 600ms; no border or shadow changes
- Navigation links: color transitions through muted intermediate before reaching gold (creates a "settling" feel)
- Cart Drawer / Quick View / Search Modal: slide-in with cubic-bezier easing over 500ms
- Accordion expand: height transition with 600ms ease-out, gold chevron rotates 180° over same duration
- Image gallery zoom: 500ms scale transition with slight delay before zoom indicator hides
- Cart success animation: button text crossfades over 400ms, gold check icon fades in, 800ms hold, reverts over 400ms

**Ambient motion:**
- Hero background: subtle Y-axis float between 0 and -8px over 6 seconds, infinite
- Statement sections: no ambient motion — let stillness speak

**Reduced motion:**
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — disable parallax, ambient float, and stagger animations
- Keep functional transitions (hover, modal open/close) but reduce duration to 200ms

## PHASE 10 — Polish, Accessibility & QA

### Accessibility audit
- Verify gold-on-dark text passes WCAG AA (4.5:1) at all sizes
- Gold at 60% opacity on near-black background may fail at body-text sizes — reserve low-opacity gold for decoration and large text only
- All interactive elements have visible focus states: gold 2px outline at 4px offset, never browser default blue
- Keyboard navigation works on all custom components: cart drawer, quick view, search modal, image gallery, color pickers, accordion sections, timeline
- All images have descriptive alt text — not just product names but visual description
- Form fields have programmatically associated labels (not just visual eyebrow labels)
- Modal/drawer focus traps: focus moves to first interactive element on open, returns to trigger on close

### Performance audit
- All product images served as AVIF with WebP and JPG fallbacks
- Hero images use `<link rel="preload">` for above-fold assets
- Aspect ratios reserved in CSS to prevent layout shift on image load
- Cormorant Garamond loaded with `font-display: swap`, preloaded
- Animations use `transform` and `opacity` only — no `width`/`height`/`top`/`left` transitions
- Cart, currency, language, reviews, recently viewed continue to persist correctly in localStorage

### Brand voice audit
- Confirm no "Buy Now" CTAs anywhere — replaced with "Add to Collection," "Discover," "Explore"
- Confirm no exclamation marks in headlines (except where intentional brand expression)
- Confirm "affordable," "budget," or discount-framing language absent from hero and featured product copy
- Confirm Spanish translations match the new lowercase, intentional tone — not just literal translations

### Content audit
- All currency formatting renders correctly across USD, EUR, GBP, MXN, COP
- Promo codes still validate
- Engraving character counter works at 20-character limit
- Recently Viewed populates correctly after viewing products
- Cart persists across sessions
- All external links have `rel="noopener noreferrer"`

### Cross-browser verification
- Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge desktop
- iOS Safari, Android Chrome
- Test all breakpoints: 320px, 375px, 390px, 768px, 1024px, 1440px
- Verify backdrop-blur renders (some Android browsers fail silently — provide solid dark fallback)

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE

A visitor lands on the redesigned homepage. The first feeling is spaciousness, not stimulation. Typography is at a scale they don't expect — large, considered, restrained. They scroll past a single statement, then meet one watch fully framed against generous black space, with a single thin gold link beneath it. The pacing feels like a magazine.

On the product detail page, the watch reveals itself slowly — each angle deliberately, each material named with care. Color swatches, the engraving option, the warranty note — all present, but all quiet.

The cart drawer slides in with a slowness that feels considered, not slow. The checkout flow uses the same restrained palette and never demands attention with badges or banners.

Mobile feels uncompressed.

When the visitor leaves, they should not recall any specific UI element — only the atmosphere of having visited somewhere considered. That is the test.
